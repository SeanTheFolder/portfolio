42 CFR Part 2 Compliance Program for SUD Records

Built and shipped the organization's 42 CFR Part 2 compliance program ahead of the February 2026 enforcement deadline. Covers substance use disorder record confidentiality, consent management, segregation of regulated records, and breach notification obligations newly aligned with HITECH.

Date
February 2026
Duration
16 weeks
Scope
Enterprise
Status
Completed

01 / The problem

The 42 CFR Part 2 final rule reached active enforcement in February 2026, expanding consent requirements, breach notification, and patient rights for substance use disorder records. The regime moved closer to HIPAA but with stricter consent rules. The organization handled SUD-relevant records across multiple systems with no segregation, no consent tracking, and no Part 2-aware audit posture.

02 / The approach

  1. Inventoried every system, workflow, and record type that touches SUD information across clinical, intake, billing, and EHR
  2. Mapped the new Part 2 requirements against existing HIPAA controls to identify the actual gap. Most of the existing program was reusable. Consent and segregation were the new work
  3. Built consent management workflows that capture, version, and revoke patient consent for disclosure of Part 2 records, with audit trails
  4. Configured Purview labeling and Sentinel detection on Part 2 record paths so disclosure events are tracked separately from general PHI
  5. Updated breach notification procedures to reflect the new Part 2 alignment with HITECH timelines
  6. Trained clinical and intake staff on the Part 2-specific consent requirements and disclosure rules
  7. Confirmed compliance posture before the February 2026 enforcement date. No scramble, no late nights

04 / What I learned

Regulatory deadlines reward early starts disproportionately. Organizations that started Part 2 readiness in 2025 had a calm February 2026. Organizations that started in late 2025 had a hard one. Treat enforcement dates as a hard target six months early, not the actual date. Also: Part 2's consent rules are stricter than general HIPAA. A clinical workflow that 'feels HIPAA-compliant' does not automatically satisfy Part 2. Walk every disclosure path with Part 2 in mind even after the HIPAA-equivalent controls are in place.
