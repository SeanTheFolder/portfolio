Anti-Phishing & Anti-Impersonation Email Defense

Eliminated domain spoofing across all organization email by enforcing DMARC, DKIM, and SPF in concert with Defender for Office 365 anti-phishing and anti-impersonation policies. PHI exfiltration via email is blocked at the gateway and reinforced by Purview DLP.

November 2024

The Problem

The organization's domain was spoofable. SPF was permissive, DKIM was unsigned, and DMARC was non-existent. There were no impersonation protections on executive accounts, no anti-phishing policies in Defender, and outbound PHI in mail had no DLP enforcement. The result was a high-trust mailbox brand with no technical controls behind it.

Approach

  1. Audited current SPF, DKIM, and DMARC posture and inventoried every legitimate sending source for the domain
  2. Deployed DKIM signing on Exchange Online and aligned SPF to the actual sender list
  3. Rolled DMARC from p=none to p=quarantine to p=reject in 30-day stages, monitoring aggregate reports between each
  4. Configured Defender for O365 anti-phishing policies with executive impersonation protection and similar-domain detection on the leadership group
  5. Wired Purview DLP into the mail flow so PHI patterns trigger blocking on outbound messages regardless of intent

Lessons Learned

Don't go straight from p=none to p=reject. Spend at least 30 days at quarantine reading aggregate reports. There is always a forgotten marketing platform or HR vendor sending mail as your domain that nobody documented. Catching those at quarantine is an inconvenience. Catching them at reject is an outage. Also: anti-impersonation policies in Defender need the actual leadership inbox list, not a distribution group. Keep that list in version control and update it as the org chart shifts.
