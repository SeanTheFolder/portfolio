Microsoft Purview DLP: Full Enterprise Deployment

Deployed Microsoft Purview DLP across every M365 workload: Teams, Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, and endpoint. PHI pattern detection blocks exfiltration at the edge while routing alerts into Sentinel for unified incident response. HITECH breach notification thresholds are monitored automatically.

Date
September 2025
Duration
4 months
Scope
Enterprise
Status
Completed

01 / The problem

PHI was moving through M365 workloads with no detection or blocking. There was no enforced DLP, no sensitivity labels, no PHI fingerprinting at the gateway. A determined or careless user could move regulated data outside the organization with no signal generated.

02 / The approach

  1. Authored sensitivity labels mapped to data classifications used by clinical, finance, and HR. Labels carry encryption and access constraints, not just markings
  2. Built DLP policies covering Teams chat, Exchange mail flow, SharePoint sites, OneDrive sync, and endpoint copy/paste with PHI pattern matching tuned to healthcare-specific identifiers
  3. Tuned to block-with-override rather than silent-block on first deployment, capturing override justifications for review
  4. Routed all DLP events to Sentinel so PHI exfiltration attempts surface in the same incident queue as identity and endpoint alerts
  5. Built a HITECH breach-notification threshold tracker so the count of records potentially exposed in any incident is visible immediately, not assembled in retrospect

04 / What I learned

DLP rolled out in silent-audit mode for too long becomes shelfware. Users learn to work around things they never see. Roll out in block-with-justification mode early so users feel the policy and you collect override reasons that tell you which legitimate workflows you missed. Tune from real signal, not theoretical models. Also: PHI patterns in healthcare are messier than the out-of-the-box templates assume. Build custom sensitive information types with the actual document samples your org generates.
