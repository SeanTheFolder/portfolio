Microsoft Teams Rooms & IoT Device Governance

Deployed and governed Microsoft Teams Rooms infrastructure across all facilities and brought the broader IoT estate under management. Intune enrolled, segmented onto a dedicated IoT VLAN, monitored through Sentinel. Shadow IoT eliminated.

August 2025

The Problem

Teams Rooms hardware was being deployed conference-room-by-conference-room with no standard. The broader IoT footprint (printers, room sensors, smart displays, AV controllers) sat on the corporate VLAN without management. Anything that plugged in worked. Nothing that plugged in was governed.

Approach

  1. Standardized on a single Teams Rooms hardware bill of materials so every deployed room behaves identically
  2. Enrolled Teams Rooms devices in Intune so configuration, updates, and compliance flow from the same console as user endpoints
  3. Designed a dedicated IoT VLAN with explicit firewall policy. IoT devices can talk to the cloud services they need and nothing else
  4. Routed IoT and Teams Rooms telemetry into Sentinel so anomalous behavior surfaces in the same incident queue as endpoint and identity alerts
  5. Wrote a 'no shadow IoT' policy backed by 802.1X and DHCP fingerprinting. Anything new that joins the network is detected within minutes

Lessons Learned

IoT governance fails when it's framed as a hardware exercise. The work is policy: what is allowed to plug in, what segment it lands on, what it can reach, what gets logged. Build the policy before the inventory exercise and the inventory exercise becomes a confirmation step instead of an open-ended search. Also: Teams Rooms hardware variation is the enemy of operational sanity. Pick one BoM and refuse to deviate, even when a department wants something cheaper.
